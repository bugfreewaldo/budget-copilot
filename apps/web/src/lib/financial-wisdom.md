## Book-by-Book Principles

### 1. Rich Dad Poor Dad — Robert Kiyosaki

**Core Principles:**

- Assets put money in your pocket; liabilities take money out. Buy assets, not liabilities disguised as assets (e.g., an overpriced house you can't afford).
- Financial literacy is more important than how much money you make. Learn to read financial statements.
- The rich don't work for money — they make money work for them through investments, businesses, and royalties.
- Mind your own business: keep your day job but start building an asset column on the side.
- The single biggest expense most people pay is taxes. Learn legal tax strategies.

**Key Quotes:**

- "The poor and middle class work for money. The rich have money work for them."
- "It's not how much money you make. It's how much money you keep."
- "An asset is something that puts money in my pocket. A liability is something that takes money out of my pocket."

### 2. I Will Teach You To Be Rich — Ramit Sethi

**Core Principles:**

- Automate your finances: set up automatic transfers to savings, investments, and bill payments so good behavior requires zero willpower.
- Spend extravagantly on things you love, cut mercilessly on things you don't. There are no "latte factor" guilt trips.
- Negotiate big wins (salary, rent, insurance) rather than obsessing over small daily expenses.
- Open a Roth IRA and invest in low-cost index funds as early as possible.
- Use the Conscious Spending Plan: fixed costs (50-60%), savings (5-10%), investments (5-10%), guilt-free spending (20-35%).

**Key Quotes:**

- "There's a limit to how much you can cut, but no limit to how much you can earn."
- "The single most important thing you can do to be rich is to start early."
- "Spend extravagantly on the things you love and cut mercilessly on the things you don't."

### 3. The Psychology of Money — Morgan Housel

**Core Principles:**

- Wealth is what you don't see — it's the cars not bought, the clothes not purchased, the money saved and invested.
- Compounding is the most powerful force in finance, but it requires patience measured in decades, not months.
- Room for error (margin of safety) is the most important part of any financial plan. Plan for things going wrong.
- No one is crazy — everyone makes financial decisions based on their unique life experience. Don't judge others' money choices.
- Getting wealthy requires optimism and risk-taking; staying wealthy requires humility and paranoia.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Wealth is the nice cars not purchased. The diamonds not bought. The first-class upgrade declined."
- "Planning is important, but the most important part of every plan is to plan on the plan not going according to plan."
- "Saving is the gap between your ego and your income."

### 4. Think and Grow Rich — Napoleon Hill

**Core Principles:**

- Desire is the starting point of all achievement — define an exact amount of money you want, by when, and what you will give in return.
- Specialized knowledge, organized and directed toward a definite purpose, is what creates wealth — not general knowledge.
- Your subconscious mind shapes your financial reality. Repeated affirmation and visualization of financial goals influence behavior.
- The Mastermind Principle: surround yourself with people who are financially smarter than you.
- Persistence is to financial success what carbon is to steel. Most people quit three feet from gold.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Whatever the mind can conceive and believe, it can achieve."
- "The starting point of all achievement is desire."
- "Every adversity, every failure, every heartache carries with it the seed of an equal or greater benefit."

### 5. Girls That Invest — Simran Kaur

**Core Principles:**

- The gender investing gap costs women hundreds of thousands over a lifetime. Start investing now, even with small amounts.
- Time in the market beats timing the market. A woman who invests $100/month from age 25 will far outpace one starting at 35 with $200/month.
- Dollar-cost averaging into index funds or ETFs removes the emotional decision-making from investing.
- Your emergency fund (3-6 months expenses) must be in place before you invest a single dollar in the stock market.
- Understand the difference between good debt (education, appreciating assets) and bad debt (credit cards, depreciating assets).

**Key Quotes:**

- "You are not behind. You are not too late. You are right on time."
- "The best time to invest was yesterday. The second best time is today."
- "Being financially literate is not optional — it's survival."

### 6. One Up on Wall Street — Peter Lynch

**Core Principles:**

- Invest in what you know: the best investment ideas come from your everyday observations — the products you use, stores you visit, trends you notice.
- Classify every stock before you buy: slow grower, stalwart, fast grower, cyclical, turnaround, or asset play. Each has different rules.
- Never invest in a company you can't explain with a crayon drawing. Complexity is not a virtue.
- The person who turns over the most rocks wins. Do your homework before investing.
- Long shots almost never pay off. Invest in boring companies with strong fundamentals.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Know what you own, and know why you own it."
- "The best stock to buy may be the one you already own."
- "Behind every stock is a company. Find out what it's doing."

### 7. The Little Book of Common Sense Investing — John Bogle

**Core Principles:**

- Own the entire stock market through a low-cost total market index fund. Don't try to find the needle — buy the haystack.
- Costs matter enormously: a 2% annual fee can consume over 60% of your returns over 50 years.
- Don't trade. The more you trade, the less you earn. Buy and hold for decades.
- Reversion to the mean is the iron rule of financial markets. Today's hot fund is tomorrow's laggard.
- The greatest enemy of a good plan is the dream of a perfect plan. Start with index funds and stick with it.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Don't look for the needle in the haystack. Just buy the haystack."
- "In investing, you get what you don't pay for."
- "Time is your friend; impulse is your enemy."

### 8. Coffee Can Investing — Saurabh Mukherjea

**Core Principles:**

- Buy high-quality companies with clean accounting, competitive advantages, and strong capital allocation — then hold them for 10+ years without touching them.
- The "coffee can" approach: invest and forget. The less you tinker, the better your returns. Inspired by the old practice of putting stock certificates in a coffee can and forgetting them.
- Focus on companies with consistently high Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) above 15% for at least a decade.
- Accounting quality is the first filter. Eliminate companies with red flags in financial statements before looking at anything else.
- Great investing is boring. Excitement and frequent trading are the enemy of returns.

**Key Quotes:**

- "The stock market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient."
- "Do nothing investing outperforms almost all active strategies over time."
- "Simplicity is the ultimate sophistication in investing."

### 9. Die With Zero — Bill Perkins

**Core Principles:**

- Maximize life experiences, not net worth at death. Every dollar you die with is a dollar you over-earned or under-lived.
- Time-bucket your life: certain experiences (backpacking, extreme sports, learning languages) are only possible at certain ages. Don't delay everything to retirement.
- Use the "memory dividend" concept: a trip taken at 30 pays dividends for 50+ years of memories. The same trip at 70 yields far fewer.
- Give money to your children when they need it most (ages 26-35), not when you die and they're 60.
- Your peak earning years and your peak health years rarely overlap. Optimize for experiences during health, not just wealth.

**Key Quotes:**

- "You should be focusing on maximizing your life enjoyment rather than your wealth."
- "Your life is the sum of your experiences. Not your bank balance at death."
- "The business of life is the acquisition of memories. In the end, that's all there is."

### 10. The Money Queen's Guide — Cary Carbonaro

**Core Principles:**

- Know your "money personality" — are you a spender, saver, or avoider? Self-awareness is the first step to financial health.
- The 72 Rule: divide 72 by your annual return rate to estimate how many years it takes to double your money (e.g., 7% = ~10 years).
- Life events (marriage, divorce, children, death) are the biggest financial planning moments. Have a plan for each.
- Insurance is not optional: life, disability, health, and property insurance protect the wealth you build.
- Women outlive men by 5-7 years on average. Plan for a longer retirement regardless of gender.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Money is emotional. Acknowledge it, then manage it logically."
- "Financial planning is life planning with numbers attached."
- "You cannot out-earn financial ignorance."

- Manage your money into designated accounts: necessities (55%), long-term savings (10%), education (10%), play (10%), financial freedom (10%), give (5%).
- Rich people focus on their net worth. Poor people focus on their working income. Track your net worth monthly.
- Rich people are bigger than their problems. Poor people are smaller than their problems. Grow yourself to grow your finances.

**Key Quotes:**

### 16. The Total Money Makeover — Dave Ramsey

**Core Principles:**

- The Debt Snowball: list all debts smallest to largest, pay minimums on everything except the smallest, attack that one with everything extra. Momentum matters more than math.
- Build a $1,000 emergency fund before doing anything else. Then build 3-6 months of expenses after becoming debt-free.
- The Baby Steps (in order): starter emergency fund, pay off all debt, full emergency fund, invest 15% of income, kids' college, pay off mortgage, build wealth and give.
- Cut up credit cards. Use cash or debit only. The borrower is slave to the lender.
- Live on a written monthly budget. Every dollar gets a name before the month begins (zero-based budget).

**Key Quotes:**

- "Live like no one else, so later you can live like no one else."
- "You must gain control over your money or the lack of it will forever control you."
- "Act your wage."
- "Debt is dumb. Cash is king."

### 17. Your Money or Your Life — Vicki Robin

**Core Principles:**

- Calculate your "real hourly wage" by factoring in commute time, work clothes, stress eating, and decompression time. Most people's real wage is shockingly low.
- Track every cent that comes into and goes out of your life. Awareness alone changes behavior.
- For every expense, ask: "Did I receive fulfillment, satisfaction, and value in proportion to the life energy spent?"
- The crossover point: when your investment income exceeds your monthly expenses, you are financially independent.
- Enough is a powerful concept. Define what "enough" means for you — more beyond that point decreases happiness.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Money is something we choose to trade our life energy for."
- "The key is not to figure out how to make more money, but to figure out how to live on less."

### 18. The Automatic Millionaire — David Bach

**Core Principles:**

- The Latte Factor: small daily expenses ($5/day = $150/month = $1,800/year = $150,000+ over 30 years with compounding) add up. But the real lesson is: automate savings so small spending doesn't matter.
- Pay yourself first automatically. If savings requires willpower each month, you will fail. Automate it.
- Own your home — over a lifetime, homeowners accumulate 40x the net worth of renters on average.
- Make all financial actions automatic: 401(k) contributions, IRA contributions, bill payments, savings transfers. Remove the human decision point.
- One phone call to increase your 401(k) contribution by 1-2% can mean hundreds of thousands at retirement.

**Key Quotes:**

### 19. The Millionaire Next Door — Thomas Stanley

**Core Principles:**

- Most millionaires don't look like millionaires. They drive used cars, live in modest homes, and avoid status spending. Wealth and income are different things.
- UAW (Under Accumulators of Wealth) vs PAW (Prodigious Accumulators of Wealth): PAWs accumulate at least 2x their expected net worth for their age and income.
- Expected net worth formula: Age x Pre-tax Annual Income / 10. Compare yourself to this.
- Millionaires budget. They track what they spend. They plan their financial lives.
- Economic Outpatient Care (giving adult children money) destroys their ability to build wealth independently.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Wealth is not the same as income. If you make a good income each year and spend it all, you are not getting wealthier. You are just living high."
- "Whatever your income, always live below your means."
- "Big Hat, No Cattle."

### 20. A Random Walk Down Wall Street — Burton Malkiel

**Core Principles:**

- Markets are largely efficient. Stock prices reflect available information. You cannot consistently beat the market through stock picking or market timing.
- Diversification is the only free lunch in investing. Spread your money across asset classes, geographies, and time.
- Dollar-cost averaging protects you from buying everything at the top. Invest a fixed amount regularly regardless of market conditions.
- Rebalance your portfolio annually to maintain your target asset allocation. This forces you to buy low and sell high.
- Avoid actively managed funds: after fees, most underperform their benchmark index over 10+ years.

**Key Quotes:**

- "A blindfolded monkey throwing darts at the financial pages could select a portfolio that would do just as well as one carefully selected by experts."
- "The market can remain irrational longer than you can remain solvent."
- "Diversification reduces risk without sacrificing return."

### 21. The Intelligent Investor — Benjamin Graham

**Core Principles:**

- Mr. Market is your servant, not your master. The market offers prices daily — you choose whether to buy, sell, or ignore. Emotional reactions to Mr. Market destroy wealth.
- Margin of Safety: only buy when the price is significantly below your calculated intrinsic value. This is the central concept of investing.
- Distinguish between investment and speculation. An investment operation is one which, upon thorough analysis, promises safety of principal and an adequate return.
- The defensive investor should stick to high-quality bonds and blue-chip stocks in a balanced portfolio. The enterprising investor can be more adventurous, but must put in the work.
- Your worst enemy in investing is yourself. Behavioral discipline matters more than analytical skill.

**Key Quotes:**

- "The investor's chief problem — and even his worst enemy — is likely to be himself."
- "In the short run, the market is a voting machine, but in the long run, it is a weighing machine."
- "The essence of investment management is the management of risks, not the management of returns."

### 26. Unshakeable — Tony Robbins

**Core Principles:**

- Market corrections (10%+ drops) happen on average once a year. Bear markets (20%+ drops) happen every 3-5 years. This is normal — don't panic.
- The biggest danger is being out of the market. Missing the best 10 trading days over 20 years can cut your returns by more than half.
- Diversify across asset classes, countries, and time. No single investment should make or break you.
- Minimize taxes through strategic use of tax-advantaged accounts, tax-loss harvesting, and understanding long-term vs short-term capital gains.
- Find a fiduciary financial advisor — someone legally obligated to act in your interest, not sell you products.

**Key Quotes:**

### 27. Financial Freedom — Grant Sabatier

**Core Principles:**

- Your savings rate is the most important variable in reaching financial independence. Saving 25% of income = ~30 years to FI. Saving 50% = ~15 years. Saving 75% = ~7 years.
- Side income accelerates everything. Build at least one income stream outside your main job.
- Calculate your "FI number": annual expenses x 25 (based on the 4% rule). That's your target.
- Optimize the Big Three: housing, transportation, and food. These represent 60-70% of most budgets.
- Every dollar you save at 25 is worth $88 at 65 (assuming 10% average returns). Urgency in your 20s pays exponentially.

**Key Quotes:**

- "It's not about the money. It's about the freedom."
- "Time is more valuable than money. You can always make more money, but you can't make more time."
- "Your income is your most powerful wealth-building tool."

**Key Quotes:**

### 30. Profit First — Mike Michalowicz

**Core Principles:**

- Traditional formula: Sales - Expenses = Profit. Profit First formula: Sales - Profit = Expenses. Take your profit first, then run the business on what remains.
- Open separate bank accounts for: Income, Profit, Owner's Pay, Tax, and Operating Expenses. Allocate percentages to each when revenue arrives.
- Small plates principle: use scarcity to drive efficiency. When you limit available operating cash, you find creative ways to spend less.
- Take a quarterly profit distribution — reward yourself for building a profitable enterprise. This maintains motivation.
- Parkinson's Law applies to money: expenses rise to meet available funds. Restrict the available funds deliberately.

**Key Quotes:**

- "Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, cash is king."
- "Don't grow your way to profit. Profit your way to growth."
- "A business that doesn't serve you financially isn't a business — it's a job with overhead."

## Universal Financial Rules

These 20 rules appear across multiple books and represent the closest thing to consensus in personal finance.

1. **Pay yourself first.** Before bills, before spending, before anything — save a fixed percentage of every dollar earned. (Clason, Bach, Ramsey, Sethi, Eker, Collins)

2. **Automate everything.** Remove willpower from the equation. Set up automatic transfers for savings, investments, and bill payments. (Bach, Sethi, Aliche, Pape)

3. **Spend less than you earn.** The gap between income and spending is the single most important number in personal finance. (Stanley, Collins, Trench, Robin)

4. **Invest early and consistently.** Time in the market beats timing the market. Compounding rewards those who start young. (Bogle, Malkiel, Kaur, Sabatier)

5. **Keep investment costs low.** High fees compound against you just as returns compound for you. Prefer index funds with fees under 0.2%. (Bogle, Malkiel, Robbins, Collins)

6. **Build an emergency fund.** 3-6 months of expenses in a liquid, accessible account before investing. (Ramsey, Kaur, Aliche, Pape)

7. **Eliminate high-interest debt aggressively.** Credit card debt at 18-25% interest negates any investment gains. Pay it off first. (Ramsey, Lowry, Collins, Trench)

8. **Diversify broadly.** Across asset classes, geographies, sectors, and time. Never bet everything on one outcome. (Malkiel, Graham, Robbins, Bernstein)

9. **Track your spending.** You cannot improve what you don't measure. Awareness alone changes behavior. (Robin, Stanley, Aliche, Eker)

10. **Live below your means.** Wealth is built by those who consistently spend less than they could, not by those who earn the most. (Stanley, Clason, Housel, Ramsey)

11. **Invest in yourself.** Your earning power is your greatest asset, especially early in life. Invest in skills, education, and relationships. (Clason, Hill, Trench, Sabatier)

12. **Don't try to beat the market.** Most professional fund managers underperform index funds after fees. Accept market returns and focus on what you can control. (Bogle, Malkiel, Collins, Robbins)

13. **Understand the difference between assets and liabilities.** Assets generate income or appreciate. Liabilities cost you money. Build an asset column. (Kiyosaki, Stanley, Graham)

14. **Protect what you build.** Adequate insurance (health, life, disability, property) prevents a single event from destroying years of progress. (Carbonaro, Aliche, Ramsey)

15. **Negotiate the big wins.** Salary, rent, insurance premiums, and interest rates have far more impact than daily lattes. (Sethi, Lowry, Pape)

16. **Know your "enough."** Endless accumulation without purpose leads to neither happiness nor security. Define what you're building toward. (Robin, Perkins, Housel)

17. **Don't let emotions drive financial decisions.** Fear and greed are the two most expensive emotions. Create rules and follow them. (Graham, Housel, Robbins, Bogle)

18. **Your net worth matters more than your income.** High earners can be broke. Low earners can be wealthy. Focus on accumulation. (Stanley, Eker, Kiyosaki)

19. **Make your money work for you.** Every dollar should have a job — either covering expenses, building emergency reserves, or invested and growing. (Kiyosaki, Clason, Ramsey, Aliche)

20. **Start now, start imperfect.** The cost of waiting exceeds the cost of mistakes. Begin with whatever you have and improve over time. (Kaur, Lowry, Sethi, Collins)

## Spending Personality Archetypes

Use these archetypes to categorize users based on their transaction patterns and provide personalized insights.

### 1. The Spender

**Emoji:** :shopping_cart:
**Description:** Spends most of their income as it comes in. Lifestyle tends to expand with every raise. Often has a low or negative savings rate and may rely on credit for emergencies.
**Strengths:** Enjoys life in the present. Often generous with others. Comfortable with money flowing.
**Blind Spots:** No financial cushion. Vulnerable to emergencies. May be spending to fill emotional needs. Often unaware of where money actually goes.
**Signals:** Savings rate below 5%. Frequent discretionary purchases. Balance low before each paycheck. Credit card balances carried month to month.
**Book Recommendation:** _I Will Teach You To Be Rich_ by Ramit Sethi — because it doesn't shame spending, it redirects it.

---

### 2. The Saver

**Emoji:** :piggy_bank:
**Description:** Consistently saves a significant portion of income. Conservative with purchases. May hoard cash in low-yield savings accounts rather than investing.
**Strengths:** Financial discipline. Strong emergency fund. Low financial stress in the short term.
**Blind Spots:** May miss out on life experiences by being overly frugal. Cash loses value to inflation if not invested. Can develop anxiety around spending even when they can afford it.
**Signals:** Savings rate above 20%. Large cash balances. Low investment allocations. Minimal discretionary spending.
**Book Recommendation:** _Die With Zero_ by Bill Perkins — to learn that money unspent and un-invested is money wasted.

---

### 3. The Investor

**Emoji:** :chart_increasing:
**Description:** Actively grows wealth through investments. Understands compound interest, asset allocation, and market cycles. Money is a tool for building, not just spending or saving.
**Strengths:** Long-term wealth trajectory. Financial literacy. Comfortable with calculated risk.
**Blind Spots:** May over-optimize and under-enjoy. Can become overconfident in bull markets. May neglect insurance or emergency funds in pursuit of returns.
**Signals:** Regular investment contributions. Diversified portfolio. Tracks net worth. Reads financial news.
**Book Recommendation:** _The Psychology of Money_ by Morgan Housel — to remember that behavior matters more than knowledge.

---

### 4. The Ostrich

**Emoji:** :see_no_evil_monkey:
**Description:** Avoids looking at finances entirely. Doesn't check bank balances, ignores bills until they're overdue, and has no budget or financial plan.
**Strengths:** Low day-to-day financial anxiety (through avoidance). Often creative and focused on non-financial pursuits.
**Blind Spots:** Small problems become big crises. Late fees, overdrafts, and missed opportunities compound. Often shocked when they finally look at the numbers.
**Signals:** Rarely opens the app. No budget set up. Overdraft fees. Unpaid or late bills.
**Book Recommendation:** _Get Good with Money_ by Tiffany Aliche — gentle, step-by-step approach that doesn't overwhelm.

---

### 5. The Optimizer

**Emoji:** :abacus:
**Description:** Tracks every cent. Compares prices obsessively. Maximizes credit card rewards, churns bank bonuses, and can tell you the exact cost of every meal this month.
**Strengths:** Deep financial awareness. Rarely wastes money. Excellent at finding deals and maximizing value.
**Blind Spots:** Can spend more time saving $5 than earning $500. Risk of analysis paralysis on financial decisions. May create tension in relationships by being overly controlling about shared money.
**Signals:** Very detailed transaction categorization. Frequent small transfers between accounts. Multiple savings goals. Uses every feature of the app.
**Book Recommendation:** _The Simple Path to Wealth_ by JL Collins — to learn that simplicity often outperforms optimization.

---

### 6. The Generous

**Emoji:** :gift:
**Description:** Gives freely to family, friends, charity, and causes. Spending on others brings more satisfaction than spending on themselves.
**Strengths:** Strong relationships. Deep sense of purpose with money. Often the person everyone turns to.
**Blind Spots:** May give beyond their means. Can enable financial dependence in others (Economic Outpatient Care). Own retirement and emergency fund may be underfunded.
**Signals:** High percentage of spending on gifts and donations. Frequent transfers to others. May have lower savings despite decent income.
**Book Recommendation:** _The Richest Man in Babylon_ by George Clason — "pay yourself first" is essential for those who naturally pay everyone else first.

---

### 7. The Debt Warrior

**Emoji:** :crossed_swords:
**Description:** Focused intensely on becoming debt-free. Every extra dollar goes toward principal payments. Lives lean and tracks payoff dates obsessively.
**Strengths:** Incredible discipline and focus. Clear measurable goal. Will build strong financial habits that last beyond the debt payoff.
**Blind Spots:** May neglect investing during prime compounding years. Can develop an unhealthy relationship with all debt (including low-interest mortgage debt that may be better kept). Risk of burnout from intense restriction.
**Signals:** Aggressive extra payments on loans. Minimal discretionary spending. Tracks debt balances frequently. Low or no investment contributions.
**Book Recommendation:** _The Total Money Makeover_ by Dave Ramsey — validates their approach and provides the framework to finish the fight.

---

### 8. The Builder

**Emoji:** :hammer_and_wrench:
**Description:** Focused on creating multiple income streams and building assets. Thinks about business opportunities, side hustles, real estate, and any way to increase cash flow.
**Strengths:** Growth mindset. Entrepreneurial energy. Often builds wealth faster than traditional paths allow.
**Blind Spots:** May take on too much risk. Can neglect personal life in pursuit of "the next thing." Might carry business debt personally. May skip the basics (emergency fund, insurance) in favor of the next investment.
**Signals:** Multiple income sources visible in transactions. Business-related expenses. Variable income patterns. Asset purchases.
**Book Recommendation:** _Profit First_ by Mike Michalowicz — ensures the building actually produces personal profit, not just revenue.

---

### 9. The Dreamer

**Emoji:** :cloud:
**Description:** Has big financial goals and talks about them often, but doesn't take consistent action. Plans to invest "someday." Will start budgeting "next month." Subscribes to financial newsletters but doesn't implement.
**Strengths:** Aspirational mindset. Often financially literate in theory. Enthusiastic about the possibility of wealth.
**Blind Spots:** The gap between intention and action is vast. May suffer from perfectionism — waiting for the "right time" that never comes. Knowledge without execution is useless.
**Signals:** App installed but rarely used. Few automated systems set up. Goals created but not tracked. Sporadic engagement.
**Book Recommendation:** _The Automatic Millionaire_ by David Bach — automation bridges the gap between intention and action.

---

### 10. The Survivor

**Emoji:** :shield:
**Description:** Living paycheck to paycheck, not by choice but by circumstance. Every dollar is accounted for by necessity. Financial stress is a constant companion.
**Strengths:** Resourceful and resilient. Expert at stretching money. Often deeply motivated to change their situation.
**Blind Spots:** Hard to see a way out when every dollar is spoken for. May feel that financial advice is "for people with money." Predatory lending and high-fee products disproportionately target this group.
**Signals:** Account balance near zero before payday. No or minimal savings. Possible payday loan or high-interest debt. Essential expenses consume 90%+ of income.
**Book Recommendation:** _Broke Millennial_ by Erin Lowry — relatable, practical, and starts from zero without condescension.

## Insight Generation Rules

When generating financial insights for users, follow these rules:

1. **Match the archetype.** Identify the user's spending personality before recommending a strategy. A Spender needs different advice than an Optimizer.
2. **Quote sparingly.** Use book quotes as occasional flavor, not constant noise. One quote per week maximum.
3. **Actionable over inspirational.** "Move $50/month to a separate savings account" beats "Start your wealth journey today."
4. **Never shame.** Frame all insights positively. "You spent $340 on dining out" is neutral. "You could redirect $100 of dining spending and still enjoy meals out 3x/week" is helpful.
5. **Context matters.** A user paying off $40K in student loans needs different advice than a user with $40K in savings and no debt.
6. **Celebrate progress.** Acknowledge when spending decreases in a category, when savings increase, or when debt decreases — even by small amounts.
7. **Use the 1% rule.** Suggest improvements of 1% or one small step. Not "save 20% of your income" but "save $20 more this month than last month."
